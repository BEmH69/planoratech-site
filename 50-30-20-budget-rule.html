The 50/30/20 budget rule: how it works and how to use it

Published 2026-10-25

The 50/30/20 rule is one of the simplest budgeting methods: split your after-tax income into three buckets and check that each one stays in range. It is a starting point, not a law.

What the three numbers mean

50 percent goes to needs: rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments and transport to work. 30 percent goes to wants: dining out, hobbies, streaming, trips. 20 percent goes to savings and extra debt repayment.

A quick example

With 2,400 euros of take-home pay, needs are about 1,200, wants about 720 and savings about 480. Write the three totals at the top of your sheet and the rest of the budget becomes a simple fill-in exercise.

When the rule does not fit

In an expensive city, needs can reach 60 percent or more. That is fine: keep savings at 10 or 15 percent and trim wants. If you are paying off high-interest debt, you can move wants down to 20 and put 30 into repayment.

How to track it

Add a column for the category (need, want, saving) next to every expense, then total each category with a SUMIF formula. A pie chart of the three totals shows at a glance whether you are on track.

Review it every month

Your life changes, so the split should too. Check the three percentages at the end of each month and move 2 or 3 points where it hurts least. Small, regular adjustments beat a big reset.
